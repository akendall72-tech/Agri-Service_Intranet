Getting in

There is no sign-up button, and that is deliberate. Accounts are created by the training department one at a time.

Getting the app

  1. Install TestFlight from the App Store. It's Apple's own app for installing apps that aren't in the store.
  2. Open the invite from the training department — an email or a link — and tap Install. Agri-Service appears on your home screen like any other app.
  3. Updates install themselves after that.

Ask the training department for an account. You'll be given an email address and a password, and you sign in with those. There's no "forgot password" link either, because the app deliberately sends no email at all. If you're locked out, the training department resets it.

On the web page, a sign-in can run out while the page is open, for instance after the computer has been asleep. The sign-in box then comes back over the page with your email filled in. Sign in again and carry on: the page is kept as it was, including an upload that hasn't been published yet.

Owners and admins are signed out of the web page after 30 minutes without using it, in every tab it's open in, so nobody at a shared computer can carry on as them. The page starts again clean and says why. Watching an online course counts as using it, and it never happens while a course is uploading. Everyone else stays signed in.

Your first sign-in

The app asks for three things before it lets you through, because all three decide what reaches you afterwards.

Set once at first sign-in

Your name
How you appear on lift plans, support requests and your forklift card. Locked once set — only the training department can change it.
Your location
Picked from the list, which includes Field Support for remote people. Locked once set — only the training department can move you.
Your groups
Service, Parts, Sales, Service Writers, Admin. Pick every one you work in. Changeable any time in Settings.

That first screen is only in the app. If you'll only ever use the web page, the training department fills in your name and location for you instead.

Why the location and the name are locked

Your location decides which administrator reviews your lift plans. If people could change it quietly, a plan could be routed to whoever was most likely to wave it through.

Your name goes on your lift plans, inspections and reports. If people could change it, someone could put a colleague's name on their own work. In Settings the name shows with a lock, like the location. (Training Status doesn't use names: it finds you by your employee number.)

The web page

Everything in the app, and the tools for running it, at agri-barn.com, in any browser on a computer, a tablet or a phone. Sign in with the same email and password as the app.

In a narrow window or on a phone, the sidebar is behind the menu button at the top left.
  • The top bar — a button for each part of the page your role opens: Admin for owners, Training for the Training Manager, Safety for the Safety Manager, and Location Dashboard for a location's admins and owners. Each opens its own side of the page with its own sidebar; the same button, now saying Exit, or Back to the app in the sidebar, comes back.

The same as the app

Every Center works on the web page too, and what's done on one shows on the other: a lift plan written on the phone can be edited on the computer, either half of an inspection can be done on either, a vehicle's weekly check, a post or an article written on one appears on both. Where the web page works differently, that Center's section of this guide says so, under On the web page.

Staying up to date

There's nothing to install. When a new version of the web page is published, a tab that's open notices within ten minutes, or as soon as you come back to it, and offers to reload. When a sign-in runs out, and when owners and admins are signed out for being away, is under Getting in.

Agri-Service's own training courses, grouped by category. Online Courses

Courses you take right there, on the web page and in the app from build 22. Each card says where you stand: Not started, In progress with when you last opened it, Passed with the date and your score, or Not passed yet. Tap it to start, resume or take it again.

The course plays full screen under a thin bar with its name and a Close button. Your place is saved as you go and again when you close it, so you can stop and pick up again on a phone or a computer, whichever is handy. A course needs a signal to play and to save your place. In the app, its pictures and videos are downloaded the first time and kept on the phone, so going back in is quicker. The forklift certification course is the first one; it's marked Forklift certification.

Built in the app from the training department's uploads. Four tiles along the top count the upcoming classes, the people registered, the open seats and the stores represented. Three tabs:

  • All Classes: every upcoming Agri-Service class with its dates, the time it starts on the clock where it's held (e.g. 8:30 AM PST), and its seats. Tap one to see who's enrolled. Email Class opens a draft to everyone registered, in BCC. With nobody set, the draft opens with no address.

Emails open in your own mail app and nothing sends until you press Send. Classes drop off once they've finished.

The Training Manager can also put a class on the schedule on the Training dashboard, and it shows here with UKG's, straight away and without an upload. Those classes have no roster until registration in the app opens.

AGCO Development Map

Where a technician is on AGCO's six levels of technical training, drawn as a road: a sign for each level and a small red tractor where you are, on its way to the next one. The road you've covered is lit green. A sign is green when the level is complete, amber when it's started, grey when it hasn't been, and red when it's required before a level you've already done. Tap or click a sign for what that level takes, the courses ticked off for you, and the courses still needed.

The button shows your six signs in miniature. Only technicians get a map, and you see only your own. Location admins see a list of their technicians first, with how many have reached each level, and owners see everyone; pick a name to open their map. In the app from build 16.

The courses come from AGCO's Technical Training Catalog, which the training department uploads, plus any it adds that the catalog doesn't list — the Level 3 online test-out, or a new class. The Training Manager ticks off each technician's completed courses on the admin page, and the map follows the ticks.

Forklift Center

Three things: your own card, the Certification Report, and the Forklift Driving Test.

Getting certified is three steps, in order: pass the online forklift course (in Online Courses; retake it if you don't pass the quiz), get the driving test done within 30 days of passing, to the end of the 30th day, and the Training Manager approves the test, which puts your card in place for three years. Until you have a card, its spot at the top of the Forklift Center shows one of three things, in the app and on the web page:

  • Get certified — the three steps, and Take the online course.
  • Driving test due — once you pass: the days left and the last day, e.g. 12 days left, get it done by Wednesday, October 21. It turns red in the last three days. You also get a notification a week before and the day before (Settings → Forklift Center).
  • Take the course again — if the 30 days ran out without a test. Pass it again and you have another 30.

Renewing works the same way, every three years: take the online course again, then the driving test within 30 days of passing it. Your card stays valid meanwhile, and the countdown shows beside it. The card says Expiring soon two months before it runs out, and the app sends a notification then and a week before, saying what's left to do.

On the web page, and in the app from build 22, with the reminders. The driving test list already follows the rule on every build, because the database decides it.

My Certification is your card: your name and photo, the truck classes you're authorised on, your location, the date it runs out, and when you passed the course. Tap the photo circle to take a picture for it. The PDF of your driving test result is there too. If you aren't certified, it says Not certified yet and shows the three steps, with the course a tap away.

The card finds you by your work email first, then by your name. If it says you're not on the list, the training department has you under a different address.

Certification Report, for location admins and owners, lists each person with their newest card: everyone at a location admin's own location, everyone everywhere for an owner. Filter to Certified, Expiring or Not valid, search by name or email, and download anyone's driving test as a PDF. The web page's Forklift Center has the same report.

Who can see a card is the database's rule, not the screen's: everyone sees their own, a location admin their location's, an owner everyone's, and whoever records a driving test sees that test.

Removing a certification

Owners can take someone's forklift certification away, the same way as a crane qualification: Remove beside them in the Certification Report, with a reason. It's never on your own.

  • Everyone sees Removed in the report, and it counts as not valid. Only owners and the person see who removed it and why.
  • The person's card says it was removed, by whom and why.
  • It comes back when an owner presses Restore, or when the person passes a new driving test. A removal only covers the card they held then, so a test recorded afterwards counts straight away.
  • The driving test PDF still shows the test's own result.

On the web page now, and in the app from build 14.

Forklift driving test

Anyone can carry out the evaluation, though it's usually the location admin.

  1. Read the Evaluator Responsibilities and tick to accept them. They set out what OSHA 29 CFR 1910.178(l) asks of whoever evaluates a forklift operator: being qualified to evaluate, observing the operator in the workplace, a truck that's been examined and is safe, running the test safely, recording only what you saw, and the certification record. The form opens once you agree, and every test asks again. Your acceptance is saved with the test and printed on its PDF beside your name.
  2. Pick the operator. Only people who passed the online course in the last 30 days and haven't had their test are listed, soonest deadline first, each with the days they have left (Dana Price · 3 days left). Their email comes with them from their account, so neither can be misspelled. You can't evaluate yourself. Someone who isn't listed needs to pass the course first, or again.
  3. Pick the truck classes they were actually tested on.
  4. Tick the four parts of the test: inspection, use of controls, manoeuvring, and driving with a load. All four have to be met for a card; anything less is recorded as not passed.
  5. Record it. The card lasts three years, which is the most OSHA allows. Your name goes on it as the evaluator, and the date they passed the online course is added.
  6. It's sent to the Training Manager for approval, and doesn't count until they approve it.

The database applies the same rule, not just the list: a test for anyone outside their 30 days is refused. A test that's turned down, or not passed, leaves them on the list for the rest of their 30 days. On the web page it's the same form, in the Forklift Center under Forklift Driving Test, for anyone.

Approving driving tests

A recorded driving test is Awaiting approval until the Training Manager (a designation, set on the admin page's People tab) approves it.

  • The Training Manager gets a notification when a test is recorded, and a count on the Forklift Center, where Driving Tests to Approve lists each one: who, when, by whom, the truck classes and the four parts of the test.
  • Each test also shows the online course behind it: when the person passed it and how many minutes after starting, both timed by the database, and the course's own clock. A pass under 10 minutes is flagged in amber, since a course reports its own pass: check they really did it before approving. No online course pass on record means the database has no pass of the forklift course for that email. On the web page once migration 0105 is run, and in the app from build 29.
  • Approve makes the card valid. Not approved needs a reason. Either way, whoever recorded the test is told by notification.
  • Someone who already holds a valid card keeps it while a new test waits. Their card and the Certification Report say a new test is awaiting approval. Someone with no card yet shows as Awaiting approval in the report.
  • Only the Training Manager decides, once per test; the database enforces it. To take a card away later, an owner removes it.
  • Rows from the spreadsheet, and tests recorded before approval was added, count as approved.

On the web page now, and in the app from build 14. The web page has the same Driving Tests to Approve page in its Forklift Center.

Crane Center

Today's crane inspections, your Crane & Rigging Qualification, and where a lift plan is written, reviewed and kept. The lists of plans show every one still waiting or sent back, and the last 12 months of the rest; Show older plans at the bottom brings back older approved ones.

Daily crane inspections

Every crane gets a short function test before its first use each weekday, as OSHA asks (the daily "frequent inspection": 1910.179 for bridge cranes, 1910.180 for truck cranes). Nothing is due on Saturday or Sunday, but a crane used at the weekend should be inspected first, and can be. It's first in the Crane Center, as Daily Crane Inspections in the app (from build 40) and at the top of the page on the web.

  • The list — the cranes at your location, each with today's state: Out of service first, then Due today, then Inspected with who did it and when. "Today" is the location's own day, so Pasco's starts an hour after Twin Falls'. At the weekend the others read Not due today.
  • Inspect — each item is answered OK or Problem, with no load on the hook: the controls and emergency stop, the hook and latch, the wire rope or chain, and for a bridge crane the hoist, the upper limit switch and the bridge and trolley travel, or for a truck crane the boom and rotation, hydraulic leaks, the safety devices and load chart, and the outriggers. A Problem needs a note saying what's wrong. Sign off records your name and the time; anyone at the location can do it.
  • A Problem tags the crane out of service until it passes an inspection again: it shows red on the list and its page says not to use it. The location's admins and the Safety Manager get a push straight away.
  • A crane's page (History) — the last 30 days as a square a day (passed, failed, not inspected, or an outlined weekend), how many weekdays it passed, and every inspection with who signed it off and what failed. It loads the last five weeks; Show older inspections brings back the rest.
  • Adding cranes — a location's admins (and owners) add each crane with a name everyone will recognise ("Shop bridge crane", "Fleet #214 crane"), its kind, bridge or service truck, which decides the inspection it gets, and its rated capacity if you know it. No two cranes in service at a location share a name. Retire takes one off the daily list and keeps its record; Delete removes it and its inspections. An admin can delete an inspection entered by mistake; nobody can change one.

Everyone sees the cranes at their own location, an admin those at every location they look after, and owners all of them; the database enforces it. The Safety Manager keeps the list of items on the Safety dashboard (Crane Inspection). A change reaches every phone at once, and an inspection already signed off keeps the items as they were worded that day.

Crane & Rigging Qualification

The top of the Crane Center says whether you're qualified for crane and rigging work. You are once the Lifting and Rigging ILT course shows on your training history — the same OLT and ILT report behind Training Status — or once the Training Manager has given you credit for Lifting and Rigging from the Course Catalog (Training dashboard → Give Credit), which goes on your record under that same name. The OLT course on its own doesn't count. It doesn't expire today, but it can: the Training Manager can set how many months it lasts from the day the course was completed (for example 24), and taking the course again starts it over. If the course is ever renamed, the name is changed in the same place, the Training dashboard's Training Status upload, and nobody has to be re-qualified.

  • Qualified — a green tile. Tap it for a card like the forklift one: the course, when you completed it, your location and when it expires ("No expiration" while it doesn't).
  • Expired — an amber alert saying when it ran out. Take the course again to be qualified.
  • Not qualified yet — an amber alert. Ask the training department when the next Lifting and Rigging ILT class is.
  • Removed — an orange alert saying who removed it, when and why. Speak to your location admin.

Location admins and owners get a Crane Qualifications list — their location, or everyone — with Remove (a reason is required, and the person sees it) and Restore, and once expiry is set, an Expired filter and each person's expiry date. Remove and Restore only work for someone who has taken the course, and never on your own qualification, so nobody can be given one the course report doesn't show. On the web page it's the last section of the Crane Center.

Lift plans

  1. Fill out the plan: crane and configuration, load weight and how it was determined, the rigging, the pre-lift inspection checklist, hazards and lift path, and a picture of the lift — up to four photos of the load and rigging as set up, taken or chosen from the library. Each is shrunk before it's saved, so it sends on one bar.
  2. Submit it. It goes to the administrators at your location, who get a notification. They can see whose it is and when it came in.
  3. They approve it (asked once more first, since it can't be undone), or send it back with a note saying what needs changing, and you get a notification either way. They cannot edit it themselves, and nobody can approve their own plan.
  4. Sent back? Edit & Resubmit, fix it, and send it again. Your location's admins are told it's back. Fixed with no signal, it says it's waiting to send, so it can't go twice.
  5. Once approved it is frozen. Nothing on it can be changed after that, which is what makes the approval mean something.

The Crosby lifting guide is in here too, and works with no signal. On the web page it's the card beside New Lift Plan: an owner uploads its PDF once, on that card, and everyone signed in can open it. It's kept in private storage, not on the public site. Any plan can be saved as a PDF, photos included, from the app or the web page.

On the web page too. New Lift Plan at the top of the Crane Center opens the same form, all on one page: the same fields in the same order, the same four things required (job description, crane type, load weight and total weight), and up to four photos chosen from the computer, shrunk the same way. The % of capacity is worked out as you type, with the same warning from 80% and over 100%. A plan you've started waits while you look at other pages: the card says Continue your lift plan. Click any plan to open it — everything on it, the photos larger, and the PDF. An admin approves it there or uses Request changes to send it back with a note; a plan of yours that came back has Edit & resubmit. A plan written on the web page shows in the app, and one from the app shows on the web page.

Crane capacity at this radius / config is a number of pounds, on the web page now and in the app from the next build (28). The % of capacity is worked out from it. It used to be words, and every digit was read as one figure, so "5 ton (10,000 lbs)" read as 510,000 and the lift looked like 1% of capacity. An older plan with its capacity in words now shows no % at all, and says why, rather than a wrong one. If one comes back for changes, the old words show under the box until a number replaces them.

Safety Center

The Safety Desk and its monthly Safety Brief, the Safety Report form for an incident, near miss, hazard or idea, Safety Reports (the reports the Safety Manager has approved, for everyone to read), and the safety data sheets. GotSafety has gone: the Safety Desk replaced it.

Safety Desk

At the top of the Safety Center, in the app and on the web page. Everyone takes the Safety Brief once a month: an OSHA document to read, then a few questions about it — multiple choice with four answers, or true or false.

  1. Open this month's brief and read it: the document, and any text under it. When the brief has a recording, Listen instead of reading plays it at the top: play and pause, a slider, and fifteen seconds back or forward. In the app it keeps playing with the screen locked, with the controls on the lock screen, and opening the PDF doesn't stop it; it stops when you leave the brief. The first play needs signal, and after that it plays without (web page once migration 0106 is run; app from build 29).
  2. Wait for the questions. They open a few minutes after you first open the brief (three, unless the training department sets it otherwise), with a countdown until then. The server keeps the time, so it carries on if you leave and come back, and changing the phone's clock doesn't help.
  3. Answer them and hand them in. Four of five right passes, unless the brief says otherwise. Didn't pass? Read it again and have another go, as many times as it takes.

Once you've passed, the brief shows Passed in green, and opening it shows your answers beside the right ones. Every earlier brief is under Your record, passed or not, to read again at any time; one you missed can still be taken. Taking a brief needs signal. In the app from build 18.

The Safety Brief reminder. The monthly Safety Brief reminder (it used to be Safety Training, for a GotSafety course) can't be ticked by hand: it ticks itself, green, when you pass that month's brief. Tapping it opens this month's brief, or the Safety Desk if it isn't up yet.

Who's taken it. Location admins and owners get the Safety Brief report on the Safety Desk: pick a month, then Not passed, Passed or Everyone, by location, with when each person passed or how many tries they've had. Admins see their own location, owners everyone; everyone else sees only their own record. Someone whose account was made after a month ended isn't expected to have taken that month's.

Safety Report

In the Safety Center, in the app and on the web page: the company's own form, in place of the Microsoft Form. Use it for a near miss, an injury, a hazard, damage, a bright idea or a safety shout-out.

  • Who you are is filled in. Your name, email and location go with the report; you don't type them.
  • Where it happened starts at your location. Pick another, or N/A if it wasn't at a location.
  • Employees involved come from the All Employees upload, so you can name someone who has no account. Type part of a name and tap them.
  • Photos from the camera or your library. Leave out faces, injuries and anything confidential.

A required question you've missed is named when you tap Send. A question takes up to 5 photos. In the app, a report sent with no signal waits on the phone and goes when signal returns, photos included.

Fleet Center

Add a vehicle asks for everything the Center needs from day one:

  • The vehicle — Pickup, Service Truck or Forklift, the fleet number (required; no two vehicles still in service can share one), an optional nickname (like Big Red, up to 30 characters), year, make, model, and the VIN and plate if you have them. The nickname shows under the fleet number on every card, and beside it wherever a vehicle is named ("Fleet #214 · Big Red"), to tell a row of F-250s apart. From build 33.
  • Meter — a forklift only: Hour meter, or No meter for one without. A pickup or service truck always has its mileage.
  • Mileage and service — the odometer, and how often it's due: every 5,000 miles or 6 months to start, whichever comes first. A forklift with an hour meter has Hours and service instead: the hour meter, and every 250 hours or so many months (leave the hours empty for the calendar alone). One with no meter is serviced by the calendar: every so many months.
  • Last service — the date and the mileage (or the hours), off the service sticker. That starts the service clock. Don't know it? Switch it off, and the clock starts with the first service you log.
  • Tire wear — Good, Fair, Worn or Replace, and the tread depth in 32nds if you measured it.
  • Equipment on it — a welder, compressor, generator, crane, pressure washer or fuel tank, with its make, model and hours.

An owner or location admin also picks who drives it. A vehicle is kept at its driver's location. Anyone else adding one is its driver.

The vehicle's page is the cab: the odometer (a forklift's hours, or No meter), then a gauge showing how much of the service interval is used. Service OK is green, Service soon amber (the last 15% of the miles or hours, or 3 weeks), Service overdue red. Under it, Mileage & maintenance (Hours & maintenance on a forklift) charts the reading from each weekly check, with each service marked on the line and a dashed line where the next one's due. Then The last check: who did it, every checklist item as they answered it, with anything needing attention first in amber with their note. Then the tires at each check, the equipment, the service log with what was spent this year, and the details.

Log service records an oil change, tires, brakes, an inspection, a repair or anything else, with the date, the mileage (a forklift's hours; nothing for one with no meter), the cost and notes. A routine Service (oil & filters) restarts the service clock from that reading and date; anything else is for the record. A reading higher than the vehicle's moves it forward.

Equipment keeps its own service clock, by hours, by months, or both (250 hours or 12 months to start). Its clock starts with the first service logged for it, so new equipment isn't shown overdue. Take it off this vehicle keeps its history.

Retire a vehicle that's sold or off the road: it leaves the lists and its Fleet check stops, but its history is kept, and Bring it back undoes it. Its fleet number can then go on another vehicle. Delete removes it with all its history.

The weekly check

Every Monday, Fleet check appears on the To-Do list of anyone who drives a vehicle. It can't be ticked by hand. On each vehicle, tap Weekly check, then give today's odometer (the hour meter on a forklift; nothing on one with no meter) and pick the tire condition. If you measured the tread, type the shallowest tire's depth in 32nds under Tread, shallowest tire; leave it empty if you didn't, and the vehicle keeps its last measurement.

Then the checklist: engine oil, coolant, lights, brakes and the like, the items for that kind of vehicle. Answer each one OK or Needs attention, and say what needs doing; the check saves once every item is answered. Add a note under Anything else? if there's something more to report. Owners keep the list on the web page (Fleet Check under Admin); a change reaches every phone at once, and a check already done keeps the items as they were worded that day.

Once every vehicle you drive has this week's check, the reminder ticks itself. Each check also adds a point to the chart. Anyone who doesn't drive a vehicle never sees the reminder.

AGCO Trivia

A quiz game about AGCO and its machines, at the bottom of the Social tab: History, Operation, Identification and Specifications to start. Every game has its own leaderboard, and every answer teaches something.

  • Join a game to go on its leaderboard, then Play a round. A round is a set number of questions, usually against a clock that drains along the top. Tap an answer (on a keyboard, 1 to 4 or A to D).
  • After every answer you see the right one and why, right or wrong, with the points it earned. That's the learning part, and the same questions come round again.
  • The end of a round gives a verdict (a perfect round gets confetti), your points, your best streak and your new place, then every question with your answer, the right one and why.
  • Leaving part-way is fine. The round waits, and Carry on with your round picks it up where it was that day. The clock doesn't stop while you're away.
  • Practice is open to anyone, any time, by category or a whole game: ten questions, no clock, and it never counts. What you know shows how often you get each category right, and which one to brush up on.
  • A game's page has the leaderboard by person or by location (each location's average per player), the rules in plain words, your rounds to look back on, and Leave the game. Leaving takes you off the leaderboard; your rounds are kept and count again if you rejoin.
  • Scores can't be made up. The database asks each question, keeps the clock and marks the answer, so a phone never has the right answer before it's been given. It's the same in the app and on the web page.

Online Courses

Under Content on the Training dashboard. Publish the course from Captivate (or any authoring tool) as SCORM 1.2, zipped, then pick the zip under Add a course and Upload the course. The page unzips it right there, reads the course's title and the page it starts on, uploads every file, and adds it unpublished. Open it to check it, then tick Published and Save to put it in everyone's Online Courses. SCORM 2004 isn't taken yet.

Each file in a course can be up to 50 MB on the current Supabase plan; the page names any that are bigger before it uploads anything. A long video usually needs a lower quality setting when it's published.

Each course has its title, a line about what it covers (shown under the title), and The forklift certification course — tick it on the one course that counts toward forklift certification. The card shows the version, when it was uploaded and by whom, and how many have passed and how many are partway. Upload a new version replaces the course for anyone starting it; anyone partway through finishes the version they started.

Each version keeps its own copy of the course's files, which counts against the 1 GB of storage on the current Supabase plan. Under Earlier versions on the course's card, Remove its files frees that room once nobody is partway through the version. Everyone's results on it stay.

Delete course, on the right of a course's card, removes the course and its files for good. Its results go with it: if anyone has taken it, it says how many, and how many passed, and asks for the course's title to be typed before it goes. Unticking Published hides a course without losing anyone's results. The forklift certification course can't be deleted while it's ticked as that course.

Reports

Under Reports on the Training dashboard, each on screen and as a PDF or an Excel workbook. Both read the same Training Status uploads as everything else.

  • AGCO Training Hours — the dealer metric for the credits report's calendar year: technicians, the share with 24+ AGCO hours against the goal, how many more it needs, and the average. Then each location, and every technician with their hours, met or how far short, and the credits that make them up. Pick Whole company or one location. The Excel file has a sheet by location, one of technicians, and one of every credit.
  • Class Report — tick one or more classes on the left, then pick Calendar year or Fiscal year (April 1 to March 31) and the year. It shows the share of technicians who took each class, the share who took all of them (and at least one), each location's share, and the people who completed them in that year, with the date of each. Only completers are listed; the percentages count everyone. A class counts when it was completed inside the year picked; the By class table also says how many took each class outside it, so an empty year isn't mistaken for nobody ever. Technicians only and the location narrow who's counted.

The AGCO hours are always the credits report's own year, because that report carries only the current one.

Mock Schedule

Under Planning on the Training dashboard. A year's classes, planned on a calendar to see how close they get the company to AGCO's dealer metric. Owners only: the technicians in it see none of it, and nothing here reaches the app, the Event Center or anyone's training record.

  • New class makes one, and you type in everything about it: its name, level, days and hours, and optionally where and how many seats. It waits under Not on the calendar until it's dragged onto a day. Double-clicking a day makes one there.
  • Drag a class to move it, onto a month along the top to send it there, or back to Not on the calendar to take it off. Classes run on weekdays, so a three-day class dropped on a Thursday runs Thursday, Friday and Monday.
  • Open a class to add technicians: everyone Training Status calls a technician, each with their hours so far and what the class would bring them to. Only those short of the hours narrows the list. Copy makes another session of the same class.
  • The top says how many technicians reach the hours with the plan, how many more the goal needs, and each location's share. Beside it is the average hours per technician (earned plus planned), and each location shows its own average; the Technicians list ends with an average row for whoever it's showing, so picking a location gives that location's. The goal starts at 65% of technicians with 24 hours; both can be changed for each year. Under each month is where the plan stands by the end of it.
  • Hours a technician has already earned that year, on the latest Training Status upload, count too. A class counts for the year it finishes in.
  • It warns about anyone in two classes at once, more people than seats, and anyone put in a Level 6 class without Level 5 complete on their AGCO map or planned earlier.
  • The Technicians list shows everyone's earned, planned and total hours. Filter to those still short, click a name to see only their classes on the calendar, or add them to a class from their row.
  • PDF in the toolbar downloads the year's plan as the Technicians list shows it with All picked: every technician, their location, hours earned, planned and in all (with how many short), and their classes in the level's colour. The score and each location's share are at the top, and every class by date (when, level, hours, technicians, where, seats) at the end. Landscape, to print or send.

Every change saves itself a moment later, and Undo (or Ctrl+Z) steps back while the page is open. Each year has its own plan; pick the year at the top. If another owner saved the same year since you opened it, the page asks whether to load theirs or keep yours.

CSV or Excel, found by heading: Part Number, Name, Description, Home Location, Quantity, then one column per field, headed with its name. The file is the whole list.

The CSV export, including the Truck Type Authorized column.

One PDF.

Every spreadsheet import has a blank sheet to download, with the right columns and example rows — except the AGCO catalog, which comes from AGCO as it is. Forklift Certifications, Payroll, Who Does What and Special Tools also offer what's in the app now, to edit and send back. Save as CSV or Excel; both upload.

Every import replaces its table rather than merging, so upload the complete file each time, not a partial one. It happens in a single step: either the new data lands or the old data stays. A half import isn't possible. The one exception is forklift: an import replaces the spreadsheet's rows and leaves every driving test recorded in the app or on the page where it is. Special tools show you every change before anything is saved, and keep the checkout history of every tool that stays.

Two useful behaviours. Somebody can hold more than one forklift record — a re-evaluation adds one rather than replacing it — and the card shows their most recent, while the older row is greyed and marked superseded in the table. And the payroll import flags any payday that doesn't line up with its own period, which is nearly always a wrong year in that cell.

When someone joins

Create their account in Supabase with their work email and a password, and give them both. They pick their name, location and groups the first time they sign in to the app. If they'll only use the web page, fill in their name and location on People; their email is there to tell which account is which. Their employee number fills itself in at the next Training Status upload if they sign in with the email UKG has; otherwise type it on People.

When a location loses its admin

An admin from another location can cover it. On People, under their location, pick it from Also covers. They keep their own location and become the covered location's admin as well: its lift plans and inspections to review, Skills sign-offs, Training Status and AGCO maps, forklift cards, crane qualifications, special tools, Safety Brief results and Community, and the notifications that go with them, such as a lift plan waiting for approval. Their own lift plans still go to their own location's admin. In the app their location shows as, say, Pasco +1. When the gap is filled, take it away with the × beside it.

Designations

Two jobs that sit on top of the owner role: Safety Manager and Training Manager. Features that belong to one of those jobs, like seeing or changing something only the Safety Manager should, will be tied to the designation, not to the owner role alone.

  • Only an owner can hold one. An owner made an admin or an employee loses theirs straight away.
  • Owners give and take them away in Admin → People, with the tick boxes on an owner's row, their own included.
  • One owner can hold both, and two owners can hold the same one.
  • Everyone signed in can see who holds which.

The Training Manager approves forklift driving tests before they count, and is notified when one is recorded. Safety Manager isn't used for anything yet.

When someone leaves

Don't delete their account in Supabase. Supabase refuses while they have lift plans, support requests or Skills history, so those can't be lost with it; only an account that never did anything can be deleted. Instead, an owner goes to Admin → People and presses Deactivate beside their name.

  • They can't sign in to the app or the web page, and they're signed out of both.
  • Their phones stop getting notifications, and any admin or owner access ends at once.
  • Everything they did stays, with their name on it. They drop off the Skills lists, and off Training Status once UKG's reports no longer list them.

They move to the bottom of People, greyed out with the date. Reactivate lets them back in with the same email and password. Owners only, and never on their own account.

Shared logins

The apparel store sits behind one password the whole company shares, and the app types it for people. Change it on the Shared Logins tab and every phone picks it up the next time that screen is opened. No new app build, nothing to reinstall. (GotSafety's login was here too, until the Safety Desk replaced it.)

These are not secrets

The app has to read a password in order to type it, and everyone in the company knows it anyway. Holding it in the database rather than in the app is about being able to rotate it in a minute.

Microsoft sign-in

Not set up yet. This is the list, in order, for when the company is ready to replace hand-made accounts with Entra ID.

What changes: people tap Sign in with Microsoft instead of typing an email and a password. Access is decided in Entra rather than by the training department creating accounts one at a time, and the company's MFA and conditional access rules come along with it. Roles stay in the app, so having a Microsoft account doesn't make anyone an administrator here.

The checklist

  1. IT registers an application in Microsoft Entra ID.
  2. IT adds a client secret and sends over the secret's Value, not its Secret ID. The Value is only shown once.
  3. IT sets the Web redirect URI to the Supabase callback below.
  4. IT turns on "User assignment required" and assigns the groups who should have the app. This is what replaces invite-only, so be deliberate about who goes on the list.
  5. Paste the client ID and the secret into Supabase, under Authentication, Providers, Azure.
  6. Set the Azure Tenant URL to the Agri-Service tenant. Without it, any Microsoft account in the world can attempt a sign-in.
  7. Add the hosted admin page's address to Supabase under Authentication, URL Configuration, in the redirect allow list.
  8. Ask for the Microsoft button to be added to the app and to the web page. About an afternoon's work.
  9. Test with one person whose account already exists, and check they land on their existing profile rather than a second one, before anyone else switches over.

Known limits

Getting it onto a phone

Through TestFlight — see Getting in. Each build lasts 90 days, and the training department uploads a new one before that, which your phone installs by itself. If the app ever says it's expired, open TestFlight and install the latest.

An Apple Wallet version of the forklift card is possible now that there's a developer account, but it isn't planned yet.

Long lists start with the recent past

Support requests, lift plans, Safety Reports, past events and the news keep everything ever written. So the app and web page don't download years of it every time they open, each starts with what's current: everything still open, and the recent past. A Show older button at the bottom of the list brings back the rest. Event sign-up lists in the app go back as far as its calendar, a month.

The web page updates itself, when you let it

The web page changes more often than the app. If a newer version is published while you have it open, a bar along the bottom says so within ten minutes, or as soon as you come back to the tab, with a Reload button; anything you haven't saved goes when you reload, so finish it first. The version you're on is at the foot of the menu. If part of a page ever fails to show, it says This page didn't load in its place and the rest keeps working; reloading usually sorts it, and if it doesn't, tell the training department which page it was.

Online courses report their own pass

As with any SCORM course, the course itself says when someone has passed, from their own phone or computer, and the database stamps the date: the date can't be typed in or moved. For an internal course with an in-person driving test after it, that's accepted, and the Training Manager sees how long after starting each pass came (above, under Approving driving tests) before approving the test.

With no signal

From build 13, the app opens with no signal however long it's been since you last used it: on a plane, or after a weekend in a dead zone. It only has to have been signed in on that phone before; signing in for the first time needs a connection. Build 12 and earlier ask you to sign in if it's been more than an hour, and can't until there's signal.

Most screens keep showing what they last loaded: your reminders, your certification card, the directory, the payroll calendar, the news, Training Status, the special tools list. Opening an article from another site, and anything else that opens a website, needs a connection.

Lift plans, support requests, replies, reminder ticks and both parts of an inspection all work with no signal. A reminder ticks straight away and shows a small cloud until the tick has sent. For the rest, the button says Save to Send Later; tap it and the item is kept on your phone. A card on the home page says how many are waiting, and they show at the top of your plan or request list marked Not sent yet. They send by themselves when signal comes back while the app is open, or the next time you open it. iPhone may also send them in the background, but not on a schedule you can count on — so if something is still waiting half an hour after you put the phone away, you get a notification.

A saved lift plan hasn't been submitted, so nobody can approve it yet. Don't start the lift until it has sent and been approved.

If the server turns one down (a plan that was approved while you were editing it, say), it stays on the card with the reason, and you choose Try Again or Discard. Signing out doesn't delete saved items. They stay on the phone and send when you sign back in on it.

Still needing signal: approving or returning plans, closing requests, adding or hiding reminders, signing off Skills competencies, checking special tools out or in, or changing them, and signing up for an event or changing who's going.

On an iPad

Everything works except calling and texting, which an iPad has no apps for. Those buttons show the number instead so you can read it off.
