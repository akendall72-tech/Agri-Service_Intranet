Who can do what

Everyone has one of three roles, and an owner can also hold one or both of two designations. What each can do is decided by the database, not by the screens: a button you don't see is a convenience, and the database would turn the change down anyway.

The three roles

Employee
Everyone starts here. Uses every Center for themselves: their own lift plans, inspections, Safety Reports, support requests, reminders, training record, certifications and the vehicles they drive. Reads what's shared with everyone: the Safety Center, news, events, Social, the directory and the special tools list.
Location admin
Everything an employee can do, and looks after the people at their location: approves their lift plans, sees and corrects their inspections, signs off their Skills, sees their training and certifications, checks special tools in and out, registers them for classes, gives them join slips and password slips, and has the Location Dashboard. An admin can also cover another location (see Bringing people in).
Owner
Everything, at every location, and the Admin side of the web page: accounts and roles, the People list, the company's settings, and the content everyone sees. A company always has at least one owner: the last one can't be made anything else.

The two designations, for owners

Training Manager
Has the Training dashboard: the Class Schedule and grading, Give Credit, the courses' training hours and record names, EPA 609, Online Courses, Training Articles, the Skills competency list and Training Settings. For an AGCO dealer, also the AGCO Development Map, the Mock Schedule and AGCO's uploads. Approves forklift driving tests before they count, and registers people for any class.
Safety Manager
Has the Safety dashboard: approves and publishes Safety Reports and is the only one who sees who sent them, writes the Safety Report form and each month's Safety Brief, keeps the required forklift and crane operator lists, the daily crane inspection checklist, the forklift certification import and the Safety Data Sheets. Gets every Safety Report the moment it's sent.

Only an owner can hold a designation; an owner made an admin or an employee loses theirs at once. One owner can hold both, and two owners can share one. Owners give them on Admin → Accounts, with the ticks on an owner's row, their own included. Everyone signed in can see who holds which.

What your company has switched on

A few parts are some companies' and not others', and an owner switches them on or off on Admin → Company, under Features:

  • AGCO dealer — everything that's AGCO's: the AGCO Development Map and catalog, AGCO's training-hours requirement (the dealer metric on Training Status, the Training dashboard and the Location Dashboard, the Mock Schedule and the AGCO Training Hours report), AGCO Academy's sessions on the Open Seat Report, and AGCO credit on courses.
  • Apparel tab — the app's tab that opens the company's apparel store, signed in.

A company without one doesn't see it at all, and nothing is deleted when one is switched off. Where this guide describes one, it says so. The company also sets its own name, logos and IT mailbox there, and the Training Manager writes its Training Center mission with the Training Articles.

Agri-Barn's platform

Agri-Barn is run by Prime Theory Labs. Its platform owner sits above every company and belongs to none: they set companies up, and place an account that's waiting for one. To help, they can open your company as its owner for up to two hours. Every change made during a visit is logged, with why they came, and your owners read the log on Admin → Company. Nobody at one company ever sees another company's people or records.

Getting in

There's no sign-up button: your company decides who's in. You get in with a join slip from your location admin, or with your work Microsoft account if your company uses Microsoft 365. Either way, you have to be on your company's People list first.

Getting the app

  1. Install TestFlight from the App Store. It's Apple's own app for installing apps that aren't in the store yet.
  2. Open the invite your company sends you, an email or a link, and tap Install. The app appears on your home screen like any other.
  3. Updates install themselves after that.

You don't need the app to start: everything is also on the web page at agri-barn.com, in any browser, with the same account.

Signing in

The app and the web page sign in the same way, with one of these:

  • Your work email and a password — the password you picked with your join slip.
  • Your sign-in name and a password — for someone with no work email: your employee number and your company, like 004217@agri-service. Your join slip shows yours.
  • Continue with Microsoft — if your company signs in with Microsoft 365. Type your work email and the button appears under Sign in.

Your first time, with a join slip

Your location admin (or an owner) gives you a slip with a code like K7QM-4XRP.

  1. On the app's sign-in screen, or on agri-barn.com, tap Have a join slip? and type the code. Capitals, spaces and the dash don't matter.
  2. Check it's your name, job and location, and see what you'll sign in as.
  3. Pick your own password, at least 8 characters, and type it twice. Nobody else ever knows it.
  4. Tap That's Me, Sign In. You're in, with your name, employee number and location already filled in.

A slip works once and lasts 14 days. Lost it? Ask for a new one: it cancels the old. If the code isn't for you, hand it back.

Your first time, with Microsoft

  1. Type your work email on the sign-in screen. If your company signs in with Microsoft, Continue with Microsoft appears under Sign in.
  2. Tap it. Microsoft's own sign-in opens, with your company's rules: your Microsoft password, and a code from the Authenticator app if your company asks for one.
  3. You're back in the app or on the web page, signed in. The first time makes your account, filled in from the People list.

If you already have an account from a join slip, Microsoft signs you in to that one, and your password keeps working too. The app forgets your Microsoft sign-in once you're in, so on a shared phone the next person isn't signed in as you.

Forgot your password

There's no "forgot password" link, because the app sends no email at all. Your location admin gives you a password slip: the same as a join slip, and you pick a new password with it. An admin's or an owner's comes from an owner. If you sign in with Microsoft, your password is Microsoft's: your IT resets it.

Your first sign-in to the app

The app asks for three things before it lets you through, because all three decide what reaches you. Whatever the People list already says is filled in for you.

Set once at first sign-in

Your name
How you appear on lift plans, support requests and your forklift card. Locked once set: only an owner can change it.
Your location
Picked from your company's locations. Locked once set: only an owner can move you.
Your groups
The groups your company works in, such as Service or Parts. Pick every one you're in. Changeable any time in Settings.

That screen is only in the app. If you only ever use the web page, an owner fills in your name and location instead.

Why the location and the name are locked

Your location decides which admin reviews your lift plans. If people could change it quietly, a plan could be routed to whoever was most likely to wave it through.

Your name goes on your lift plans, inspections and reports. If people could change it, someone could put a colleague's name on their own work. (Training records don't use names: they find you by your employee number, then your work email.)

Staying signed in

The app keeps you signed in. On the web page a sign-in can run out while the page is open, for instance after the computer has been asleep: the sign-in box comes back over the page with your email filled in, and once you sign in again the page is as you left it, an upload not yet published included.

Owners and admins are signed out of the web page after 30 minutes without using it, in every tab it's open in, so nobody at a shared computer can carry on as them. Watching an online course counts as using it, and it never happens while a course is uploading. Everyone else stays signed in.

An account that's switched off is signed out of both, at once.

Bringing people in

For owners, and for location admins with their own people. One idea runs through all of it: the People list decides who's in; signing in only proves you're someone on it.

How it fits together

The People list
Everyone who works for the company: name, employee number, work email, job and location. Admin → People → People list.
Ways onto the list
The All employees upload from your HR system, your Microsoft 365 directory, or by hand.
Ways in
A join slip, or Sign in with Microsoft. People with no work email get a sign-in name.
The gate
Every new account is checked by the database before it's made. Only a join slip, or a Microsoft sign-in by someone on the People list, gets through. Nobody can sign themselves up, and no one outside the company can get in by having a Microsoft account.

The app sends no email at all, so there are no invitations or reset links to go astray or land in spam. People are matched to their records by employee number, then work email, never by name.

The People list

Owners see everyone; a location's admins see their own people on their Location Dashboard → Join slips. Each person shows where they came from (Upload, Microsoft or By hand) and whether they have an account yet. The chips along the top show who's Here, who has No account yet, who was Added by hand, and who has Left.

Nobody is ever deleted from it. Someone who drops off is marked as left and comes back if they return. Undo it, beside the last change, puts the list back as it was before.

Keeping the list up to date

Pick one way to keep it current; anyone the others don't have can be added by hand.

  • The All employees upload — your HR system's export of everyone (at Agri-Service, UKG's All employees report), uploaded by an owner on the People list itself, under All employees upload, whenever people join, leave, change job or move. Before it's published it says who would join, move location and leave. It also keeps Training Status's list of people, so a company whose People list comes from Microsoft 365 keeps uploading it for that.
  • Microsoft 365 — the list fills itself from your Microsoft directory, every night. See The People list from Microsoft 365 below.
  • By hand — owners, on the People list: Add someone by hand, for a new hire, a contractor or anyone the feed doesn't have yet. They need an employee number or a work email. A feed never takes off someone added by hand, and takes them over if it lists them.
The guard

An upload or a night's sync that would take off more than a tenth of the list (at least 3 people), or everyone at a location, stops and asks first. The usual cause is the wrong file, such as one location's export instead of everyone's. If it really is everyone, tick that it is.

Join slips

  1. Owners: Admin → People → People list. A location's admins: Location Dashboard → Join slips, for their own locations' people.
  2. Press Join slip beside someone with no account, or Join slips for all under No account yet.
  3. The codes show only then: Print slips lays them out to cut apart, or copy them, before leaving the page. Only a fingerprint of each code is kept, so nobody can look one up later.
  4. Hand each person their slip. They follow Getting in.

A slip lasts 14 days and works once; giving a new one cancels the old. A switched-off account can't be given one. After 10 wrong codes in an hour from one place, the join page stops answering it for a while.

Sign-in names. Someone with no work email signs in with their employee number and the company's short name, like 004217@agri-service. Their join slip shows it, and that's all they need to remember besides their password.

Password slips

For a forgotten password. Beside someone who already has an account, the button says Password slip. It works like a join slip, and the person picks a new password with it. A location admin gives them to the employees at their locations; a password slip for an admin or an owner comes from an owner.

Sign in with Microsoft

For a company on Microsoft 365. Its people sign in with their work Microsoft account instead of a password of ours: one less password, your Microsoft rules (such as a code from the Authenticator app) apply, and someone switched off in Microsoft can no longer sign in with it. Join slips keep working alongside, for anyone without a Microsoft account.

Setting it up takes an owner and one person in your IT who is a Global Administrator (or Privileged Role Administrator) of your Microsoft 365. Nothing has to be typed into Microsoft by hand.

  1. An owner opens Admin → Company → Microsoft 365 and presses Make a link for IT, then Copy. The link works once, for a week.
  2. Send it to IT. If you're the administrator yourself, open it yourself.
  3. IT opens it, signs in to Microsoft, reads what Agri-Barn is asking for (below) and presses Accept. Microsoft may say the app is from an unverified publisher; that's expected.
  4. Their browser comes back to agri-barn.com saying Microsoft 365 is connected, with the email domains Microsoft gave it. Microsoft sign-in is now on.
  5. Make sure everyone is on the People list with the same work email Microsoft has for them.
  6. Tell people to type their work email and press Continue with Microsoft.

What IT is approving

Sign people in
Microsoft tells Agri-Barn who someone is: their name and email address.
Read the directory
Names, work emails, job titles, departments, offices, employee IDs, and whether each account is switched on. Only used if you keep the People list from Microsoft.
Read group membership
Only to tell who's in a group, if you choose to take the People list from one group.
Read the organization
Your verified email domains, so nobody has to type them and nobody can claim one that isn't yours.

Agri-Barn never sees anyone's Microsoft password and never changes anything in Microsoft. Your email domains come only from Microsoft: not even an owner can type or change them.

Each person needs to be on the People list with the same work email, and to have an email address in Microsoft at one of your verified domains. Someone with a mailbox has one. An account with no licence may not: in the Microsoft Entra admin center, open the user, Edit properties, and fill in Email under Contact information.

Switching it off. Untick Sign in with Microsoft on Admin → Company to stop it for now; anyone whose account was made with Microsoft then needs a password slip to sign in. Disconnect forgets your Microsoft 365 altogether; the accounts stay.

The People list from Microsoft 365

Once Microsoft 365 is connected, the People list has a From Microsoft 365 card, so the list keeps itself up to date from your directory instead of an upload.

  • Who's on the list — everyone in the directory, or the members of one Microsoft group (handy for leaving out shared mailboxes and outside accounts).
  • Location comes from — the Microsoft field that says where someone works: Office, Department, City or Company name. Its value is matched to a location by the location's name or any of its other names on Groups & Locations.
  • Employee number comes from — Microsoft's Employee ID, or not from Microsoft.
  • Check what it would do reads Microsoft and shows who would join, come back, move and leave, any location from Microsoft that doesn't match one here, and who was left out: guests, accounts switched off in Microsoft, and anyone with no email address there.
  • Update the list now makes the change, and Undo it takes it back.
  • Keep the list up to date every night does it by itself at about 2 AM Mountain, with the choices saved under Save these choices. A night the guard stops says so on this card, for an owner to check and update by hand.

When someone joins

  1. They're on the People list: from the next upload, the next night's Microsoft sync, or added by hand today.
  2. They get in: a join slip from their location admin, or Continue with Microsoft.
  3. Their name, employee number and location come from the list. In the app they pick their groups.
  4. If they're to be a location admin or an owner, an owner changes their role on Admin → Accounts.

When someone leaves

The upload, or Microsoft, marks them as left on the People list. Their account is switched off by either of these:

  • By hand — an owner presses Deactivate beside them on Admin → Accounts.
  • By itself — with Switch off the account of someone who leaves the list ticked on the People list, an account is switched off when its person leaves the list, and back on if they return. Never an owner's. It's off unless an owner turns it on.

A switched-off account can't sign in, is signed out of the app and the web page, gets no more notifications, and loses any admin or owner access at once. Everything they did stays, with their name on it. Reactivate lets them back in as they were. Don't delete an account in Supabase: it's refused for anyone with records, so nothing can be lost that way.

Roles, names and numbers

On Admin → Accounts, an owner changes someone's role (never their own, and never the last owner's), gives designations, and fills in the name and location of someone who only uses the web page. Employee # is what finds someone's training records; it fills itself in from the People list by work email, so type it only for someone who signs in with a different email from the one on the list. No two accounts can share one.

Covering a location

When a location loses its admin, an admin from another location can cover it. On People, under their location, pick it from Also covers. They keep their own location and become the covered location's admin too: its lift plans and inspections, Skills sign-offs, training, certifications, special tools, Safety Brief results, join slips, and the notifications that go with them. Their own lift plans still go to their own location's admin. In the app their location shows as, say, Pasco +1. When the gap is filled, take it away with the × beside it.

Other ways in

These aren't offered yet. Ask Prime Theory Labs when a company needs one:

  • Sign in with Google — for a company on Google Workspace, set up the same way as Microsoft.
  • SAML single sign-on — for Okta, OneLogin, ADFS and the like. It needs a paid plan, and it can't take over accounts people already have, so it suits a company starting fresh.
  • Automatic provisioning (SCIM) — the People list kept up to date by your identity system, for a company whose HR or IT system speaks it.

The web page

Everything in the app, and the tools for running it, at agri-barn.com, in any browser on a computer, a tablet or a phone. Sign in the same way as in the app.

Finding your way

  • The sidebar — Home, then the Centers (Training, Reminders, Forklift, Safety, Crane, Support, Inspection, Social and Fleet), then Company: HR, Who Does What, News, and Help and Support, which opens in a new tab: who to ask, and this guide. In a narrow window or on a phone, the sidebar is behind the menu button at the top left.
  • The top bar — a button for each part of the page your role opens: Admin for owners, Training for the Training Manager, Safety for the Safety Manager, and Location Dashboard for a location's admins and owners. Each opens its own side of the page with its own sidebar; the same button, now saying Exit, or Back to the app in the sidebar, comes back.
  • Home — the same Centers in the same groups as the app, with your next payday, your reminders, upcoming events and the latest news.

The same as the app

Every Center works on the web page too, and what's done on one shows on the other: a lift plan written on the phone can be edited on the computer, either half of an inspection can be done on either, a vehicle's weekly check, a post or an article written on one appears on both. Where the web page works differently, that Center's section of this guide says so, under On the web page.

Only on the web page

  • Admin, for owners — accounts, the People list, the company's settings, locations, reminders, the forms, Trivia and the rest: see The admin page.
  • The Training and Safety dashboards, for the Training Manager and the Safety Manager. (The Location Dashboard is in the app too.)
  • Uploads — All employees, on the People list, and for an AGCO dealer, AGCO's Technical Training Catalog on the Training dashboard.
  • Printouts and spreadsheets — the Location Dashboard's PDF report, the monthly Safety Report PDF, the AGCO catalog's courses as a CSV file (AGCO dealers), and the import templates.

Only in the app

  • Notifications — pushes go to the phone. The web page shows the same red numbers while it's open.
  • Working with no signal — the app keeps inspections, lift plans and the forms on the phone. The web page needs a connection.
  • The first sign-in screen, where you set your name, location and groups. For someone who only uses the web page, an owner fills those in.
  • Apparel, for a company that has switched it on.

Staying up to date

There's nothing to install. When a new version of the web page is published, a tab that's open notices within ten minutes, or as soon as you come back to it, and offers to reload. When a sign-in runs out, and when owners and admins are signed out for being away, is under Getting in.

Home

The Centers are dark tiles over a photograph of a tractor in stubble, grouped by what you're doing. Each has its own colour, in the stripe down its edge and in its icon, so you can find it before you've read its name:

  • Your day — Reminders, as a wide row.
  • On the job — Crane, Inspection, Forklift and Safety, and a wide Fleet Center row under them.
  • Learning & help — Training and Support, and for a location's admins and owners a wide Location Dashboard row under them.

Location Dashboard is the web page's Location Dashboard in the app: tiles for the location, a To do list (tap one to see who), the Safety Brief and Safety Reports for this month or last, EPA 609, forklift cards and crane qualifications, each with the Safety Manager's required operators, Certifications, upcoming classes, and the location's Fleet. For an AGCO dealer it leads with the location's AGCO dealer metric on a ring against the goal, and who's short of their AGCO hours. An admin covering another location, and an owner, pick the location at the top. Pull down to refresh.

The Certifications card counts who at the location holds a DOT medical card, a welding certification, CPR or first aid, and how many are expiring or expired. Tap it for who has what: each person with every certification they hold and when it runs out, forklift, crane and EPA 609 included. The chips along the top show one certification at a time, soonest to run out first; Only what's expiring or expired narrows it to the ones to chase. View card opens the picture someone added.

A red number on a Center means something is waiting on you: reminders still to tick, a lift plan to review or one that came back, an approval you haven't looked at, or new support replies. The same total shows on the app's icon on your phone's home screen, and clears as you deal with them.

A Safety Manager gets a number on the Safety Center: Safety Reports waiting for their approval. It's in the number on the app icon too, and on View a Safety Report inside, and it goes down as they're approved on the web page. The web page's Safety Center tile shows the same.

The Crane Center's number adds the cranes at your location still to be inspected today, Monday to Friday, to any lift plans waiting on you.

The Fleet Center's number is the vehicles you drive that need something: this week's check not done, service overdue, tires to replace, something on the last check needing attention, or equipment overdue.

Location admins and owners also get a number on the Forklift Center: driving tests recorded since they last opened it. Opening the Center clears it, on every device. It isn't added to the number on the app icon.

Company news is on the News tab.

On the web page, home looks the same: the red greeting, then your next payday, reminders, upcoming events and anything waiting, and the same Center tiles in the same groups, with Social beside Reminders under Your day. On a computer the photograph is a wider one. Beside the tiles is Latest news: the three newest stories, and All news for the rest. Looking at them there doesn't clear the red number on News; opening News does.

The Centers

Each Center, and the HR tab, lists what's in it as dark cards on a page that fades from white to grey: a colour down the left edge, a line about what it's for, and in capitals at the bottom, what tapping it does. An arrow pointing up and out instead of a chevron means it leaves the app, for a website or an email. On the cards, red is kept for the numbers that mean something is waiting on you.

The web page's Centers look the same, under a red band with the Center's name. The admin pages keep their plain look.

Training Center

The course catalog, Online Courses, Skills, the open seat report, My Classes, Training Status (whose Classes tab, for location admins and owners, is who has taken a class), and the AGCO Development Map — the wide button across the bottom of the links. Also the articles under Read up: the technician training requirements for the current AGCO pathway, and the institutional syllabus. The Training Manager edits those on the Training dashboard, and they're saved on the phone once opened. Everything in the Training Center is built into the app; training assigned to you in another system (at Agri-Service, UKG ProLearning) stays in that system.

My Classes

The classes you're registered for, in the app and on the web page. The Open Seat Report's rosters carry everyone's work email, and yours is matched to the one you sign in with. The next class is at the top, with how long until it starts; each shows its dates, start time, town and instructor. Add to my calendar puts it in your calendar (in the app you pick which one; on the web page it downloads a file to open in Outlook).

It's current: register for a class and it's on My Classes straight away, with Cancel my seat. A full one you're waiting for is under On the waitlist, with your place and Leave the waitlist. Once a class is completed, Your results lists it with passed or not passed and your score, if there was one. AGCO Academy classes aren't listed, as their report has no rosters. Nothing coming up? It points you to the Open Seat Report to find one.

You also get a notification a week before each class and the day before. Settings → Training Center switches them off.

Skills and Training Status have their own sections below.

Course Catalog

Your company's own training courses, grouped by category. Search by course, equipment or topic, and use Who it's for to show only the courses for Service Technicians, Parts Associates, or anyone else a course has been set up for. Tap a course for its level, credits, length, description, key topics and prerequisites. The catalog is saved on the phone, so it opens with no signal. At the bottom, Questions? names the Training Manager, with their email to tap.

Online Courses

Courses you take right there, on the web page and in the app. Each card says where you stand: Not started, In progress with when you last opened it, Passed with the date and your score, or Not passed yet. Tap it to start, resume or take it again.

The course plays full screen under a thin bar with its name and a Close button. Your place is saved as you go and again when you close it, so you can stop and pick up again on a phone or a computer, whichever is handy. The bar says when it last saved, and once you pass, Passed — it's on your record. Opening a course you've finished starts a fresh go; your pass stays on your record.

A course needs a signal to play and to save your place. In the app, its pictures and videos are downloaded the first time and kept on the phone, so going back in is quicker. The forklift certification course is the first one; it's marked Forklift certification.

Open Seat Report

Your company's own classes, put on the Class Schedule by the Training Manager, and for an AGCO dealer, AGCO Academy's sessions from AGCO's catalog. Four tiles along the top count the upcoming classes, the people registered, the open seats and the stores represented. Three tabs:

  • All Classes: every upcoming class of your company's with its dates, the time it starts on the clock where it's held (e.g. 8:30 AM PST), and its seats. Tap one to see who's enrolled. Email Class opens a draft to everyone registered, in BCC. Request Registration asks who needs a seat, then opens a draft to the Training Manager (whoever holds the designation on People) with the class filled in. With nobody set, the draft opens with no address.

Forklift driving test

Anyone can carry out the evaluation, though it's usually the location admin.

Crane Center

Daily crane inspections

Every crane gets a short function test before its first use each weekday, as OSHA asks (the daily "frequent inspection": 1910.179 for bridge cranes, 1910.180 for truck cranes). Nothing is due on Saturday or Sunday, but a crane used at the weekend should be inspected first, and can be. It's first in the Crane Center, as Daily Crane Inspections in the app and at the top of the page on the web.

  • The list — the cranes at your location, each with today's state: Out of service first, then Due today, then Inspected with who did it and when. "Today" is the location's own day, so Pasco's starts an hour after Twin Falls'. At the weekend the others read Not due today.
  • Inspect — each item is answered OK or Problem, with no load on the hook: the controls and emergency stop, the hook and latch, the wire rope or chain, and for a bridge crane the hoist, the upper limit switch and the bridge and trolley travel, or for a truck crane the boom and rotation, hydraulic leaks, the safety devices and load chart, and the outriggers. A Problem needs a note saying what's wrong. Sign off records your name and the time; anyone at the location can do it.
  • A Problem tags the crane out of service until it passes an inspection again: it shows red on the list and its page says not to use it. The location's admins and the Safety Manager get a push straight away.
  • A crane's page (History) — the last 30 days as a square a day (passed, failed, not inspected, or an outlined weekend), how many weekdays it passed, and every inspection with who signed it off and what failed. It loads the last five weeks; Show older inspections brings back the rest.
  • Adding cranes — a location's admins (and owners) add each crane with a name everyone will recognise ("Shop bridge crane", "Fleet #214 crane"), its kind, bridge or service truck, which decides the inspection it gets, and its rated capacity if you know it. No two cranes in service at a location share a name. Retire takes one off the daily list and keeps its record; Delete removes it and its inspections. An admin can delete an inspection entered by mistake; nobody can change one.

Everyone sees the cranes at their own location, an admin those at every location they look after, and owners all of them; the database enforces it. The Safety Manager keeps the list of items on the Safety dashboard (Crane Inspection). A change reaches every phone at once, and an inspection already signed off keeps the items as they were worded that day.

  • Meter — a forklift only: Hour meter, or No meter for one without. A pickup or service truck always has its mileage.
  • Mileage and service — the odometer, and how often it's due: every 5,000 miles or 6 months to start, whichever comes first. A forklift with an hour meter has Hours and service instead: the hour meter, and every 250 hours or so many months (leave the hours empty for the calendar alone). One with no meter is serviced by the calendar: every so many months.
  • Last service — the date and the mileage (or the hours), off the service sticker. That starts the service clock. Don't know it? Switch it off, and the clock starts with the first service you log.
  • Tire wear — Good, Fair, Worn or Replace, and the tread depth in 32nds if you measured it.
  • Equipment on it — a welder, compressor, generator, crane, pressure washer or fuel tank, with its make, model and hours.

An owner or location admin also picks who drives it. A vehicle is kept at its driver's location. Anyone else adding one is its driver.

The vehicle's page is the cab: the odometer (a forklift's hours, or No meter), then a gauge showing how much of the service interval is used. Service OK is green, Service soon amber (the last 15% of the miles or hours, or 3 weeks), Service overdue red. Under it, Mileage & maintenance (Hours & maintenance on a forklift) charts the reading from each weekly check, with each service marked on the line and a dashed line where the next one's due. Then The last check: who did it, every checklist item as they answered it, with anything needing attention first in amber with their note. Then the tires at each check, the equipment, the service log with what was spent this year, and the details.

Log service records an oil change, tires, brakes, an inspection, a repair or anything else, with the date, the mileage (a forklift's hours; nothing for one with no meter), the cost and notes. A routine Service (oil & filters) restarts the service clock from that reading and date; anything else is for the record. A reading higher than the vehicle's moves it forward.

Equipment keeps its own service clock, by hours, by months, or both (250 hours or 12 months to start). Its clock starts with the first service logged for it, so new equipment isn't shown overdue. Take it off this vehicle keeps its history.

Retire a vehicle that's sold or off the road: it leaves the lists and its Fleet check stops, but its history is kept, and Bring it back undoes it. Its fleet number can then go on another vehicle. Delete removes it with all its history.

The weekly check

Every Monday, Fleet check appears on the To-Do list of anyone who drives a vehicle. It can't be ticked by hand. On each vehicle, tap Weekly check, then give today's odometer (the hour meter on a forklift; nothing on one with no meter) and pick the tire condition. If you measured the tread, type the shallowest tire's depth in 32nds under Tread, shallowest tire; leave it empty if you didn't, and the vehicle keeps its last measurement.

Then the checklist: engine oil, coolant, lights, brakes and the like, the items for that kind of vehicle. Answer each one OK or Needs attention, and say what needs doing; the check saves once every item is answered. Add a note under Anything else? if there's something more to report. Anything needing attention shows on the vehicle's page, its card in the list, the Fleet Center's number and the Location Dashboard's To do, until the next check. Owners keep the list on the web page (Fleet Check under Admin); a change reaches every phone at once, and a check already done keeps the items as they were worded that day.

Once every vehicle you drive has this week's check, the reminder ticks itself. Each check also adds a point to the chart. Anyone who doesn't drive a vehicle never sees the reminder.

Moving up

The moment the last competency in your level is signed off, you move up to the next one and get a notification. Finishing a level is permanent: if a competency is added to that level later, it shows as added after this level was finished and doesn't move you back.

A competency above your level can be signed off early — if you do a Level 3 job while you're on Level 2, it can count now. It won't move you up on its own, but it's already done when you get there.

Things you might see

For location admins

Your technicians and parts people

Adding someone

  1. Skills → Add a technician, or Add a parts person under Parts.
  2. Pick the person. Only people at your location who aren't on that list are listed.
  3. Choose the level they're working on now. Someone new to the program starts at Level 1. Someone already working at Level 3 goes in at Level 3, and Levels 1–2 are recorded as placed, so you don't have to sign off 69 things they did years ago.

Signing off

  1. Open the technician and pick the level tab.
  2. Tap Sign off next to the competency you watched them do.
  3. Add a note if it helps — the machine, the job, the work order. Then confirm.

Your name and the date are stamped by the system, not typed, so the record can't say someone else signed it. Sign off when you've actually seen it done. It's the whole point of the program.

Sign-offs need signal

AGCO Trivia

A quiz game about AGCO and its machines, at the bottom of the Social tab: History, Operation, Identification and Specifications to start. Every game has its own leaderboard, and every answer teaches something.

  • Join a game to go on its leaderboard, then Play a round. A round is a set number of questions, usually against a clock that drains along the top. Tap an answer (on a keyboard, 1 to 4 or A to D).
  • After every answer you see the right one and why, right or wrong, with the points it earned. That's the learning part, and the same questions come round again.
  • The end of a round gives a verdict (a perfect round gets confetti), your points, your best streak and your new place, then every question with your answer, the right one and why.
  • Leaving part-way is fine. The round waits, and Carry on with your round picks it up where it was that day. The clock doesn't stop while you're away.
  • Practice is open to anyone, any time, by category or a whole game: ten questions, no clock, and it never counts. What you know shows how often you get each category right, and which one to brush up on.
  • A game's page has the leaderboard by person or by location (each location's average per player), the rules in plain words, your rounds to look back on, and Leave the game. Leaving takes you off the leaderboard; your rounds are kept and count again if you rejoin.
  • Scores can't be made up. The database asks each question, keeps the clock and marks the answer, so a phone never has the right answer before it's been given. It's the same in the app and on the web page.

For owners

The admin page's Trivia tab has three parts:

  • Games. A title, description and prize; the first and last day; which categories it draws from (none ticked means all); how long a round is (3 to 50 questions); the time for each question (10 seconds to 2 minutes, or no clock); and the points for a right answer. Good to know

A few things that make the app and the web page easier to live with.

The app keeps itself up to date

The app comes through TestFlight for now (see Getting in). Each version is good for 90 days, and a fresh one arrives before then and installs itself. If the app ever says it's expired, open TestFlight and tap Install on the latest.

So does the web page

There's nothing to install. When a newer version is published while you have it open, a bar along the bottom says so within ten minutes, or as soon as you come back to the tab, with a Reload button: finish what you're typing first, then reload. The version you're on is at the foot of the menu. If one part of a page ever doesn't show, it says This page didn't load in its place and everything else keeps working; a reload usually sorts it, and support can help if it doesn't.

Long lists open fast

Support requests, lift plans, Safety Reports, past events and the news keep everything ever written. So they open quickly, each starts with what's current: everything still open, and the recent past. Show older, at the bottom of the list, brings back the rest, as far back as it goes. Event sign-up lists in the app go back as far as its calendar, a month.

Online course passes are stamped

An online course reports its own pass, as any SCORM course does, and the database stamps the date, so nobody can type it in or move it. For the forklift course, the Training Manager also sees how long each pass took before approving the driving test (see Approving driving tests).

Working with no signal

The app opens with no signal however long it's been since you last used it: on a plane, or after a weekend in a dead zone. All it needs is to have been signed in on that phone before.

Screens keep showing what they last loaded: your reminders, your certification card, the directory, the payroll calendar, the news, Training Status and the special tools list.

Lift plans, support requests, replies, reminder ticks and both parts of an inspection all work with no signal. A reminder ticks straight away and shows a small cloud until the tick has sent. For the rest, the button says Save to Send Later: tap it and the item is kept on your phone. A card on the home page says how many are waiting, and they're at the top of your plan or request list marked Not sent yet. They send by themselves when signal comes back while the app is open, or the next time you open it. iPhone may also send them in the background, and if something is still waiting half an hour after you put the phone away, you get a reminder.

A saved lift plan goes for approval once it sends, so start the lift once it has sent and been approved.

If the server turns one down (a plan that was approved while you were editing it, say), it stays on the card with the reason, and you choose Try Again or Discard. Signing out keeps saved items: they send when you sign back in on that phone.

These wait for signal: approving or returning plans, closing requests, adding or hiding reminders, signing off Skills, checking special tools out, in or changing them, opening a website or an article from one, and signing up for an event or changing who's going.

On an iPad

Everything works on an iPad. The call and text buttons show the number instead, since an iPad has no phone or messages app to open.
